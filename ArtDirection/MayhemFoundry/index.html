What changed during development

  • Rectangular ramp toes initially intersected supports; tapered toes removed that penetration.
  • The combs needed wider load-bearing spines to keep the cantilever mass over their bearings.
  • Movement testing exposed switchback headroom failures. Separate ramp lanes and open landings replaced overlapping flights.
  • Bridge entrances moved away from solid columns. Portal street junctions became single H-shaped sections.
  • Arches required flared feet, tied movable cradles, raised keystone bearing seats and a targeted 32/8 solver. Lower settings kept creeping.
  • The harbor needed sixteen velocity iterations to settle its hinges; its position solver remains at 12.
  • Shared visual and convex meshes reduce repeated geometry allocation/cooking. The map uses original colliders without a second survivor-collision world.

What to play-test first

My design picks are the bookends, portal quarter and skyrail: they produce stacked routes and covered space from relatively few moving sections. The moving harbor and keystone vaults have more distinctive failure events, but require more careful bearings and solver settings. The drums are a useful wild card because a fallen wall can become a floor. These are design judgments to test with players, rather than a claim that automated movement proves fun.

All districts keep normal gravity and contact response. The harbor leaves use real heel bearing stops, with hinges joining them to movable frames rather than the world. Lower and upper stair pads provide space for capsule movement to finish a slope before turning. Loose bridge plates can still form small obstacles that require going around, jumping or climbing.

Measured isolated stability

Every final district starts without penetrations deeper than 4 cm, settles with no body moving more than 0.6 m, and has all bodies asleep at the 15-second measurement. Most initial movement is loose links seating onto their bearings. Supports and weights were then removed without forcing a collapse animation.
