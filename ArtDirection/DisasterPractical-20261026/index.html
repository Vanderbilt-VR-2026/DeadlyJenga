Assessment after inspecting the actual renders

These are technical studies, not finished replacement art. The opaque tornado exposes its cone; the solid wind strips look like ribbons rather than air. I would reject the ribbon treatment as finished art. A next pass should blend a shaped core with a small, measured number of soft, textured wisps and a fading crown.

The turquoise wave shading reads more clearly, but the rectangular footprint, hard water contact and smooth crest remain obvious. A next pass needs ocean-edge blending, broken foam shapes and restrained crest spray. Preserve the gameplay surface underneath; do not promise a fully overturning wave from a material change.

These intentionally simple prototypes establish a lower-complexity baseline, not Unity's visual ceiling. The earlier generated cloud volume and foam density are still unproven in this game.

What this proves — and what remains

The shader and mesh approach can render inside this Unity project with its existing renderer. These are frozen Editor art studies with shader UV motion available; they do not prove gameplay integration, motion quality, stress-test FPS or Quest performance.

All four share one 256 × 256 packed 2D texture with mipmaps. There are zero 3D textures, volumetric effects, refraction passes, particles or added colliders. Shadows on the effects are approximated with surface shading; the effects do not cast shadows. The live game still uses its original disasters.

Next would be choosing a direction, hooking the visual to the existing disaster state, checking moving edges and ocean blending, then comparing GPU/frame time in gameplay and on the headset. If soft mist is needed, it should be a separately measured, tightly capped addition.
