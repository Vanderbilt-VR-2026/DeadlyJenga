    How these themes would stay visual and economical

    Keep every collider, rigidbody, slab, pillar, passage and map boundary. Give large panels, narrow posts, tops and undersides different surface treatments instead of assigning city-building functions. Concentrate sparse ornaments at exterior corners and back faces; leave walking surfaces, grip faces and sight lines clear. Use a small shared opaque material family, surface textures, restrained emissive masks and an environment preset. Keep top faces readable and quieter than side faces; preserve block seams so loose pieces remain understandable after collapse. Nothing shown here has been performance-tested or applied in Unity.

    Jungle motifs can start as bark, moss and leaf-litter surface patterns; neon as emission rather than hundreds of lights; water as a palette and surface treatment; stars, planets, cloud banks and distant silhouettes as backdrop art. Sparse ornaments would attach to existing pieces without their own physics bodies or colliders. They still add rendering cost and need a measured budget. Gloss, water, lighting and shader costs still need measurement on device.

    The concepts retain the existing water boundary as a visual reference. Alternate no-ocean presentations should retain that boundary and clearly depict the danger, rather than making a lethal fall look like safe solid ground. Frost, apparent rain, lava patterns and amber seas add no new gameplay hazards or traction changes.

    For night themes, use enough ambient fill to read other players and the undersides of decks. Team colors and objective markers must remain distinct from the environment; color alone should never identify the route or danger.

    Natural reference sources

    These are original shape-led proposals. Natural materials and optical patterns provide references; the structures are abstract playgrounds, not literal city buildings.

    • NOAA: bioluminescence — inspiration for glowing ocean and organic surface motifs.
    • National Park Service: White Sands geology — gypsum whites and mineral texture for Sunbleached Salt Circuit.
    • NASA: evaporation ponds near Moab — saturated blue water against pale mineral terrain; the pink salt-ocean proposal is a creative extrapolation.
