FESTIVAL CITY RUNTIME OPTIMIZATIONS — 24 OCTOBER 2026

USE
Select Festival City - Optimized. Original Festival City and Packed Shapes are preserved. This new copy retains the same 633 independent structural bodies, authored parts, masses, solver settings and ramp/room geometry. No global project physics settings, packages or saved team scenes were changed.

IMPLEMENTED
Core/Services: the explicitly approved ITraversalCollisionWorld.UsesOriginalColliders read-only capability.
World/Maps and Blocks: original structural colliders provide character collision on the new map, removing all 1,049 duplicate map survivor shapes and their copy-update work. Ordinary blocks and compound rooms register their actual colliders. Global collision policy restores on map exit. Original maps retain their existing proxies and masks.
Characters/Player: world-aware walking, grounding, climbing, vault and ledge-look masks; active-mode capsule layer configuration before roomscale motion; bounded overlap recovery for dynamic walls, since the native CharacterController does not provide equivalent dynamic recovery by itself. The recovery helper's capsule collider is disabled and only used for explicit penetration computation. Other maps retain their old query policy.
Characters/Bots: native transform/property calls in compound and ramp sampling are replaced by per-shape matrix/rotation/size/center reuse. Sampling positions and face/long-span coverage are retained. Navigation/ceiling/route queries follow the explicit world collision capability, including newer working-project walking-route and planner code.
Presentation/Visuals: runtime instancing for repeated room meshes and submeshes, with ordinary renderer fallback for unique or low-count models, material property blocks, unsupported instancing or API failure. Only groups with at least three instances are batched. Materials are cloned; original resources remain untouched. Source material properties are propagated. Gameplay remains independent of this component.
World/Maps: room-aware debris adapter for all generated members. It uses full compound bounds and the existing 18m/s impact / 20m/s fast-debris policies, with a 2s creation grace period, displacement/contact checks, conservative player/hand/view guards, bounded swept motion and observer-triggered native restoration. Displaced unoccupied debris reaching static ground can sink at 2m/s. Burial cancels if new structural contact or a nearby observer appears. Stable body indices remain. Gameplay impact announcements use existing Core events; no particles, sound or UI are emitted by World.
Cleanup is conservative in multiplayer: more than one human disables these retirement/ballistic decisions until a complete remote occupancy policy is available. Native physics remains active. This avoids deleting geometry near unobserved remote people; it also means the single-player cleanup saving is not promised online.

LIMITS AND FOLLOW-UP
Short unfocused laptop Editor samples with other Unity processes active, not standalone or Quest benchmarks. Wall-clock wave windows and budgeted bot decisions do not make physics trajectories identical. Absolute FPS and small differences are noisy. The earlier estimated 10–16 FPS result did not materialize consistently; the final stress pair was 8.2 to 9.3 FPS.
ADB reported no connected device. No headset tracking/controller, Quest GPU/thermal, live two-player or latency/loss session was tested. VR-mode fixtures exercise the production motor in Editor; they are not a headset claim. Network wire formats and actor kinds were not changed. Replica/authority behavior and stable world indices were exercised locally.
The remaining bottleneck is native simulation and catch-up during active contact, with additional bot/query and allocation costs. This patch preserves current solver/CCD settings and collapse behavior. It does not claim Quest readiness or solve the 72 FPS target.
