WHAT WAS BUILT
Crown of Giants / Crown Archipelago: stacked giant pylons and wide bearing caps carry
loose, long catwalks. Alternating intermediate connections create different routes on
successive tiers. Six crown districts chain together in the grand map.
Terrace City / Terrace Mountain: broad stacked masses form low-contact-count ascending
ridges around a courtyard. Roof seams, cover and perimeter notches break up the surface.
Falling Viaduct / Ribbon Skyways: a grid of slimmer pylons at staggered heights supports
individual spans. Changing upper connections makes support loss affect different routes.
Hollow Megakeeps / Megakeep District: four corner piers, crossed lintels and ring floors
create hollow towers, joined across multiple levels. Nine towers form the grand district.
Festival of Giants: an outer keep-and-viaduct circuit surrounds inner crown crossings and
low terraces. Intermediate bearing seats split long spans. Physical sloped ramps and
steps provide two different ground approaches. The layout uses varied rectangular sizes;
ramps are rotated solid boxes with matching collision geometry. No triangle meshes are
claimed in this version.

WHAT THE PHYSICS TESTS TAUGHT US
Every member is an independent dynamic, gravity-driven production CastleBlock. Only the
sand foundation is static. There are no bonds, stabilizing joints, magnetic attraction,
scripted collapse triggers, or supported-body kinematic shortcuts.
All nine layouts passed deterministic generation, no initial penetrations, horizontal
bearing checks, eight seconds of native settling, standing spawn clearance, and deliberate
support-loss response. Ramps additionally settled at their authored rotation; the overlap
check uses oriented boxes rather than treating their bounding boxes as solid geometry.
All bodies were asleep after settling. Maximum observed drift was 0.1488 m; maximum tilt change was 1.585 degrees.

Crown: removing one ground pylon displaced 13 members in the study. Strong cascade with
few bodies; broad caps provide good routes, but giant pieces resist a weak local impact.
Terrace: the corresponding removal displaced one upper mass. Best containment and lowest
wave cost in these samples. The tradeoff is less dramatic chain collapse and fewer narrow
escape routes. A giant moving mass can also trap space rather than make small debris.
Viaduct: removing one support displaced eight members in the study, 13 in the grand.
Most exposed chain-collapse routes, but narrow piers and long spans leave fewer recovery
options. A tsunami waking the whole grid remains expensive despite the modest count.
Keep: one missing corner stayed up through redistributed bearings. Removing the adjacent
corner displaced 41 study members / 67 grand members. This is useful structural redundancy,
not failed destruction. Costs more bodies and creates tall wall/climb sections.
Hybrid: one corner survived; two adjacent corner losses displaced 59 members. Initial
settling drift was 0.0715 m. Mixing durable low routes with vulnerable high routes provides
more variation than using every district with the same failure behavior.
These tests demonstrate physical responses. Fun, route mastery and balanced game rounds
still need human playtesting; they cannot be established by a support-removal script.

RECOMMENDATION
Start human comparison with Terrace City, Crown of Giants and Falling Viaduct. Terraces
provide the most economical large playable area in these observations; crowns give stronger
mayhem per support failure; viaducts add exposed traversal. Use keeps as selective landmarks,
rather than filling every district with them. Festival is the ambitious mixed experiment.
The grand maps intentionally explore scale and are not performance-certified shipping maps.
The remaining spike problem needs separate profiling of whole-map hazard wakeups, character
collision surfaces and rendering/scripts. Sparse architecture reduces the number of moving
members but does not itself make hundreds of simultaneously awake members VR-safe.
