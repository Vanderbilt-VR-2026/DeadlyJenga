ALTERNATIVE 01

Tideway Metropolis

Best overall starting point

An S-shaped dry tidal boulevard cuts diagonally through a compact city on one continuous oval island. Low waterfront sheds and open markets in the foreground rise through courtyard apartments and parking terraces to a single tall gateway-office pair at the rear. Two unequal intermediate landmarks, a branching Sky Tree and a city-hall lantern, interrupt the skyline. Several broad mid-height crossings stitch BOTH bends of the S together. This is a clustered asymmetrical coastal city with distinct low and high neighborhoods, NOT twenty detached towers.

Routes, collapse behavior, ingredients and risks

How it plays

The S street is the readable low route; a counter-running mezzanine loop crosses it at 8–16 m; scarce high bridges at 24–32 m offer shortcuts. Taller roofs are rewards, not the only connection.

How it changes

Buildings lean toward two generous pocket courts off the S bends. Loose short crossings may become ramps or cover, but a separate route outside each fall area stays available. Keep one high district usable after a local collapse.

Existing ingredients to adapt

Main risk

Low–medium relative to the set; paired gateway must not dominate all sightlines.

ALTERNATIVE 02

Double-Loop Exchange

Best route redundancy

A large bow-tie urban block forms two unequal open courtyards on one island. The smaller seaward court is lower, sunny and wide; the inland court is taller and partly sheltered. Two off-center exchange towers flank the narrow waist, with an open ground arch AND a mid-level crossing. Apartment galleries, hotel wings, ramped garage terraces and a crown bazaar form incomplete perimeter blocks with multiple street gates. Clearly visible figure-eight circulation at different heights, not a solid wall ring.

Routes, collapse behavior, ingredients and risks

How it plays

Each court has its own ground loop and mid-height loop. Two spatially separate waist crossings connect them; an outside coastal lane avoids both. Crossing heights differ to prevent one sightline controlling everything.

A court wing can fill one part of one loop while the other courtyard remains a viable arena. A lost upper waist bridge removes a shortcut but cannot sever the entire map.

ALTERNATIVE 03

Caldera Switchback City

Best continuous ascent

A broad dry bowl inside a continuous island, surrounded by a broken spiral of stepped open buildings. The spiral rises from a low seaward opening to two unequal inland skyline peaks. Make it a terraced hillside city, not a pyramid or wedding cake: rectangular courtyards, cut-through porticos, branching ramps and abrupt breaks in the spiral. Crownline office at one high end, Saddle Beacon at the other, broad parking terraces halfway up, and a low cathedral-like arcade partly crosses the bowl.

Routes, collapse behavior, ingredients and risks

How it plays

A segmented, legible ascent follows inhabited terraces, with several short down-and-across chords. Two switchback stairs and a climbing rib connect each major terrace; no enormous smooth continuous ramp is assumed.

How it changes

A low-side section can settle downslope into the empty bowl. Upper pieces may become slopes but can also jam: leave cross-bowl ground exits and broad receiving space. Avoid a funnel where all debris reaches one pinch point.

Main risk

Medium–high: slopes invite persistent sliding; test each terrace and contact seam before multiplying them.

ALTERNATIVE 04

The Broken Crown Capital

Best clear landmark and arena

An incomplete faceted ring of urban buildings surrounds a large offset civic square, with three generous radial street openings and an off-center 60m lantern tower. Ring buildings vary strongly in height: a low bazaar, a medium U-shaped hotel, one tall office wedge and a hollow cathedral gateway. Only selected floors bridge gaps; the whole ring is not one solid structure. Outer low market canopies and a terraced shoreline form a second loose circulation band.

Two incomplete rings linked by diagonal streets make several loops. The civic square has offset cover islands rather than one empty firing field; upper crossings reveal only pieces of it.

ALTERNATIVE 05

Harbor Loom

Best unusual interconnected layout

A waterfront city woven from three broad inhabited ribbons: a low shipping arcade, an intermediate offset viaduct, and a higher line of roof streets. They braid by crossing at separated heights, never as an impossible knot. Open foundry halls, a waterworks lantern, unequal office bars and short branch-mesa crowns occupy the crossing nodes. The entire sandy shoreline is ONE connected island. Parallel urban bands curve gently along a shallow dry dock and have frequent cross-streets, not an endless narrow bridge.

Peak proposal 48 mUrban footprint 330 × 220 mAuthoring ceiling 86 bodies / 920 shapes / 0 joints
Routes, collapse behavior, ingredients and risks

How it plays

Fast routes run lengthwise, protected short routes cut through halls, and broad crosslinks occur near each node. A ground street follows every ribbon, so losing the viaduct never strands a whole neighborhood.

How it changes

Short simply supported bridge spans fall independently between broad seats. A collapsed span can create a screen or incline at a dock edge. Avoid long rigid bars colliding with half the map or a vast suspended joint chain.

Main risk

Medium: too many crossing surfaces can become visually confusing; height and shape cues must be unmistakable.

ALTERNATIVE 06

Lantern Ravine

Best dramatic vertical combat

Two broken terraced urban ridges enclose a sinuous dry ravine on one island. The left ridge is a lower stepping housing quarter; the right ridge rises into three unequal office/hotel landmarks. Three very different crossing points: a broad low arcade, a mid-level inhabited saddle and one high gateway bridge. A waterworks crown anchors the seaward mouth. The ravine floor has cover, turnoffs, side steps and daylight; it is a playable street, not an empty deadly canyon. A peripheral coast route connects both sides.

ALTERNATIVE 07

Canopy Borough

Best open-and-sheltered mixture

A dense but porous island city beneath offset branching structural canopies. Three large Y-crown buildings at different heights interleave with a taller office lantern, low foundry sheds, a court apartment block and chunky stepped terraces. Under the overlapping upper wings are shaded streets and open column courts, not forest scenery. The canopy is interrupted by tall daylight slots and generous missing pieces, so players can read both the skyline and streets. Several upper roofs make a broken branching promenade.

Three lower hinged inhabited book-wings face broad side courtyards; tall independent city hall and zipper office landmarks rise at opposite ends. Three Hearts City

Best for varied game modes

A coherent triangular coastal city with three unequal connected civic neighborhoods around a shared low market crossing. One is a tall gateway/business district, one a medium lantern courtyard quarter, and one a broad low foundry/parking waterfront. Two overlapping street loops cross through the shared center, while offset roof bridges connect neighboring districts directly. Buildings sit along shared streets and courts, not on three separate pads or islands. A few portal frames and folded terraces knit the seams.

ALTERNATIVE 10

The Rising Spine

Best large-scale skyline journey

A diagonal stepped city-street spine rises across a continuous island from a low seaside market to a tall inland civic headland. It meanders in a broad zigzag and branches through neighborhoods on BOTH sides. Short portal viaducts, roof terraces, office steps, hollow courts and low vaulted halls create a continuous urban section, punctuated by two distinct secondary towers. Some sections cross over the low street, creating covered underpasses; clear side escape alleys prevent a wall-like barrier. Do not make a single gigantic solid ramp or one repetitive line of blocks.

Architecture, gameplay and physics design rules

Build neighborhoods around movement

Compose 6–9 dominant building masses with shared street edges and local courts. Include a 0–4 m street band, 8–16 m mezzanine band and selective 24–32 m crossings. Only a few landmarks reach 48–64 m. Connect neighboring roofs directly where practical; a player should not descend to sand after every building.

Make height change frequent, not compulsory

Use roughly 4 m floor intervals from the tested kit, with useful rest landings and clear stair openings. Give each main district two independently supported ascent routes. Prefer 3–5 m circulation widths and broad turning pads; verify using the actual scaled capsule. Secondary expert jumps must never be the sole connection.

Climbing faces must outrun floor edges

On designated exterior ascent faces, continuous vertical ribs extend beyond the slab envelope; floor bands sit behind them. Remove the Festival red-lip obstruction as collision geometry, not just as a color change. Review images are not precise collision drawings; some illustrated edges still need this correction in authoring.

Short routes between layers

The current source defaults include 4.6 m/s desktop walking, 1.82 m jump height and a 38-degree slope-grip angle; settings, VR mode and effects can change these. At that baseline 20 m is about 4.3 seconds and 300 m is about 65 seconds before obstacles. Avoid an enormous empty exhibition island: concentrate encounters within connected neighborhoods and reserve the beach as a perimeter, not mandatory commuting.

Shelter comes from useful solids

Pillars alone do not provide reliable gunfire or disaster shelter. Add a few large wall panels, deep porticos and roofed corners per district. Keep several open sides and daylight slots. Check actual game shelter queries separately from whether an image looks shaded.

Use separately stable physical sections

Start with two- or three-floor cassettes and aligned, broad real bearings. Keep each upper section's projected center of mass within its support polygon at rest. A bridge sits on both end seats and is not an invisible tensile brace. No magnetic support, floating anchor or giant static structural core is assumed.

Design fall space, then test it

Reserve 15–30 m local receiving courts beside lower vulnerable wings, with at least two spatially separate detours. These courts do not guarantee containment of a 60 m tower. A whole-stack topple can sweep much farther: run whole-building impacts in multiple directions and reject layouts that regularly seal every exit. Collapse biases come from mass, supports, gaps and catches, not scripted teleporting.

A tilted thing is not automatically a good route

A 6 m rise over 12 m horizontal run is about 26.6 degrees; the same rise over 6 m is 45 degrees. The latter may slide under current defaults. Bookfall's previously observed roughly 67.6-degree stop is a climb/cover experiment, not a comfortable walking ramp. Give rubble broad usable faces, and never require a specific accidental landing to finish a route.

Limit cascading contact regions

Stagger break seams and leave expansion gaps so neighboring stacks do not rest against one another. Use a few short loose bridge spans rather than island-wide beams or long joint chains. Shared walking connectivity should not require every district to be mechanically locked into one giant contact network.

Retain meaningful ruins

Keep large settled cassettes while they provide cover or routes. Small fragments may be brief visual effects; any later cleanup must respect standing players, objective items and required connections. Do not promise that instantly deleting most rubble will preserve the desired mayhem.

Control views with form

Give each district a recognizable silhouette and opening pattern, supported by color. Bend streets and offset upper galleries to interrupt map-wide shots. Use daylight courts for orientation and a few deep bays for hiding. More upper floor area must not turn into complete surveillance of all low routes.

Targets are ceilings, not FPS predictions

Research → concrete design decisions

Primary sources below support the factual summaries. Applying them to loose candy structures is our design reasoning, not an engineering validation by the original architects or game studios.

Steven Holl Architects — Linked Hybrid

What the source supports: The architects describe a porous complex with passages at ground level and a connected upper loop of skybridges.

Our design application: Use independent low and middle circulation networks, with several ways between them. Borrow spatial organization, not the building's real structural system.

Safdie Architects — Habitat 67

What the source supports: The project combines stepped modules, terraces and continuous elevated pedestrian streets. Its modules are structurally connected using systems including post-tensioning and welding.

Our design application: Let a roof become the next district's street, but replace real cantilevers and structural ties with visible broad compression bearings or rigid fabricated cassettes. Loose game blocks cannot inherit the real project's tensile behavior.

MVRDV — Valley

What the source supports: The complex combines unequal towers with public ascent into an elevated valley and a covered street-like atrium.

Our design application: Treat negative space between buildings as the main level: a low street, covered grotto and raised court are useful playable places, even without enclosed rooms.

Embark — THE FINALS Update 7.3.0, destruction section

What the source supports: The update describes knock-on building damage, keeping heavily damaged level design playable, and clearer damage visuals.

Our design application: Judge intact, partly damaged and heavily damaged layouts separately. Use readable section seams and receiving spaces. This is a design precedent, not an assertion that our engine has Embark's destruction features.

EA / DICE — Battlefield Labs: Destruction

What the source supports: DICE frames destruction as a way to alter routes and tactical cover, with clear visual/audio language and testing of collateral damage.

Our design application: Every major failure should change a choice: open a view, interrupt a shortcut, reveal a court or create cover. Do not optimize only for dramatic total flattening.

NVIDIA — PhysX Best Practices

What the source supports: The guide discusses simplifying scenes, reducing shape complexity, difficult jointed systems, sleeping piles, and divergence between platforms.

Our design application: Budget shapes, contacts, joints and awake bodies alongside body count. Avoid huge hinge networks and unsupported performance promises. Its native SDK advice is conceptual guidance; Unity does not expose every option directly.

Unity — enhanced physics performance

What the source supports: Unity recommends physics profiling and using suitable collision geometry and simulation settings.

Our design application: Verify costs in the production Game scene before expanding the approved composition. A visually simpler map can remain CPU-bound for reasons outside its structures.

Earlier local studies also referenced GDC talks. GDC Vault returned a maintenance page in this research pass, so no new conclusions here depend on inaccessible talk content.

Build and validation plan for the selected map

Implement one selected composition, starting with a connected slice. Preserve the existing exhibition maps as a library and comparison baseline. This review task does not alter the game.

  1. 1 · Prove a connected slice

    Build three adjacent building masses, one local court, two ascent systems, one supported bridge and the low street. Target no more than 32 structural bodies / 320 shapes / 0 joints. Verify traversal, contacts and collapse before cloning.

  2. 2 · Establish the two route networks

    Author the low route and middle loop with the actual player capsule. Remove each shortcut and ascent in turn. Test bidirectional travel, controller/headroom clearance, and an exterior climb across every floor band.

  3. 3 · Add the skyline selectively

    Expand toward the chosen map's ceiling, with one dominant peak, two secondary peaks, low waterfront buildings and deliberate open gaps. Preserve the existing original maps; add a new map ID only when implementation is requested.

  4. 4 · Validate physical aftermath

    For each district: remove a support, hit low and high from several directions, lose a bridge, then apply production tsunami. Use multiple runs/seeds and intact/partial/heavy damage snapshots. Report actual accessible routes and rubble contact counts; do not substitute generated aftermath art.

  5. 5 · Profile the whole production game

    Run the same camera and settings at 0, 16, 32 and 50 bots; separate idle, normal tsunami and severe impact. Record main-thread physics time, contacts, awake bodies, bot/query time, draw calls, allocations, frame-time median/p95/p99 and reset behavior. Measure real-player movement through rubble. Compare to existing maps in controlled runs.

  6. 6 · Balance modes and device comfort

    Use travel-time measurements for CTF spawn/flag choices, multiple contest heights for hill modes, shelter distribution for disasters and tighter active districts for Murder Mystery. Test real two-player networking and a Quest headset before claiming device readiness. Avoid forced camera movement on collapsing floors.

Acceptance gates before calling the map finished
  • Zero significant initial penetrations, no spontaneous long-term creep and sleep behavior comparable with the proven isolated prototypes.
  • Each main neighborhood reachable from two independently supported approaches before damage; no single high bridge is the only city connection.
  • After representative local failures, remaining objectives/spawns remain reachable or are handled explicitly by mode rules. Heavy whole-city destruction is assessed separately.
  • Stair holes, turning pads, landing lips and climb ribs checked with real motors, not only geometry sampling.
  • Default tsunami judged independently from the stronger demolition impulse. Existing sturdy sections do not automatically become wave-sensitive.
  • Structural allowances met, then actual production frame times and device comfort measured. No approval based on body count alone.
Ideas deliberately left out of the first build
  • Importing all 20 full towers plus all Foundry districts: repeats the exhibition sprawl and greatly increases contact/shape load.
  • Magnets to hold the city together: unnecessary for the proposed compression-supported forms and would add tuning and support behavior.
  • Real-building cantilevers copied as loose blocks: architecture precedents often rely on reinforcement, post-tensioning or fixed connections absent in our loose physics.
  • Rolling Borough as a foundation for occupied high towers: a moving base adds instability and navigation cost. Its curved silhouette can inspire a low isolated feature later.
  • Counterweight Sky Harbor as a city-wide mechanism: preserve its tiered harbor silhouette first; defer its jointed balancing mechanism.
  • One keystone that destroys the whole map, one mandatory summit route, or promises that every collapse makes a perfect bridge.
