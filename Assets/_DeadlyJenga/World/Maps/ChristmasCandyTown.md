# Christmas Candy Town

A fixed, flat Christmas town for comparing game modes without a collapsing Jenga structure. Choose **Christmas Candy Town** in the lobby or Record Matches map selector. Recommended population: 16 player slots. Use a walkable island rule and disable automatic disasters when measuring baseline bot behavior.

The 96 × 76 metre map has sixteen enterable buildings: the original shop/civic row and eight smaller homes. Shops retain 2.6 metre doors and 3.8 metre headroom; the smallest homes have 1.82 metre doors and more than 3.2 metres of headroom. Every building has front and rear access and a clear central through-route. Windows are actual openings. Sugarplum Family Diner has red vinyl booths, a soda counter, fixed stools, checkerboard flooring, pie plates and an old radio. Maple Corner Grocery has produce crates, stocked pantry shelves and a family-owned storefront. Snowbell Elementary has six desks, chairs, exercise books, chalkboards, cubbies, children's drawings and a bell tower. The bakery, toy workshop, bookshop, post office, town hall and four Christmas market stalls remain neighborhood businesses. Homes have beds, quilts, hearths, dining furniture, porches, mailboxes, evergreen planters and fenced back gardens. Footprints, stacked firewood, sleds and local notices add everyday details without simulated town NPCs. Family drawings, kitchen cookie jars and boots by the door carry the lived-in details into the homes. Founders Family Park has an open bandstand and benches; the schoolyard has chalk hopscotch and a covered play shelter. Low picket fences have open gates, and painted street crossings link the community spaces to the neighborhood. Play equipment is fixed scenery, with no swinging joints or moving parts.

The central square connects a cross street, market lanes, house shortcuts and an outer snowy loop. Cherry and mint pads mark the two team ends. Authoring validation checks capsule connectivity from the plaza to every interior, both community spaces and all four spawn points, as well as standing room and through-door clearance. Market counters and usable furniture have fixed collision; small decorative props and footprints have none.

World owns all solid collision. Architecture and decoration have no simulated dynamic bodies. Seven fixed kinematic pads implement block ownership so Color Control has claimable surfaces without movable blocks. Characters still use normal collision and movement. The town is not a physics-free replacement for testing character movement, combat or networking.

The central objective pad is the highest claim surface for hill testing. CTF uses the normal mode's team-base selection. Survival has little vertical variation by design; this map does not exercise tower collapse, moving-block traversal or high-altitude escape. Disasters may still move or affect characters when enabled. Turning off automatic disasters is a match setting, not a hidden map override.

The visual prefab and materials live in Presentation/Visuals/ChristmasTown. It reuses glossy cherry, mint, lavender and icing colors alongside the same selected subset of Kenney's Holiday Kit; the expansion needs no additional downloads. Runtime winter atmosphere hides the generic tropical sand/ocean while this map is active, then restores them when it is removed. Removing Presentation leaves World collision and objective pads playable. Static layout synchronization is version 4; older geometry is intentionally rejected.

Open **Christmas Candy Town Preview** for an isolated art preview; it contains no player or game session. Use the lobby to play modes. **Tools → Deadly Jenga → Maps → Build Christmas Candy Town** explicitly rebuilds this map's prefabs, meshes and preview. It never saves Game.unity or Lobby.unity. It is not run automatically on import. Existing source props stay unchanged; combined meshes reduce decorative draw calls.

**Tools → Deadly Jenga → Validation → Check Christmas Town Runtime** runs a short real Game-scene CTF smoke check with 20 bots and restores the previous scene setup. Movement measurements exclude initial placement and large respawn jumps. Authoring checks cover all sixteen interiors, doorway capsule sweeps, route connectivity, shelter and the static geometry boundary. Reports and overview, street, market, neighborhood, diner, school, grocery, park, schoolyard and interior captures go to Logs/Validation. These checks do not establish headset comfort, Quest performance, peer synchronization or completed-round strategy.

## Evening art and match foundations

The visual prefab owns a blue-hour sky, cool directional light, amber porch lanterns, interior fills and Christmas tree lights. Shared 256px repeating textures and small normal maps give the gingerbread baked grain, timber a wood grain and paving staggered stone joints. Cube UVs use physical size, and decorative mesh batches are split into spatial regions so local lights and culling remain useful. Desktop gets a gentle bloom/color grade. At runtime the local-light budget is sixteen on desktop and six in VR; the town volume is disabled in VR. This is a budget choice, not a measured Quest performance claim. The component restores previous environment, camera and sun settings when removed and yields to lighting disasters.

`GameSession/Modes/MapMatchLayout` is reusable match setup data. The town's resource asset binds to the exact Core ISyncedWorld layout token, rather than referencing a World class. Its local coordinates follow the map root transform. It supplies distributed free spawns, eight team areas with separate flag homes, and a hill objective. MatchModeRuntime selects clear, unoccupied candidates first and uses the existing procedural placement on maps without a matching asset. New map layouts can author the same data without changing bot logic or Core. The prefab also contains named position-only markers for inspection.

Authoring validates ground support, capsule clearance and connected routes for every spawn and objective. Two teams use the west/east gates; additional teams use north/south lanes and garden approaches. The seven ownership pads remain available to Color Control. For a baseline test use Walkable island rules and disable automatic disasters; Survival and Infection can otherwise apply their normal sinking/lethal rules. These foundations do not imply competitive balance for every team count or testing of every mode's complete round logic.

The winter sky is a project skybox with stars, a moon and a subtle static aurora. A snow carpet, rolling snow hills and a pine belt conceal the background beyond the neighborhood. These are Presentation scenery outside the playable bounds, with no extra collision or expansion of the gameplay area.

Light snowfall uses a small native particle system. Its shader samples an editor-authored, conservatively expanded shelter-height texture, generated from the map's actual static collision. Flakes are discarded below roofs/canopies and outside the neighborhood. This also blocks wind-blown flakes from appearing through open windows. There are no particle physics collisions or per-flake raycasts. Rebuild the map after changing shelter geometry so the mask stays current. The mask follows the map transform.
