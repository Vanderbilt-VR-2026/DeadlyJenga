# Full-size death collapse and demo inspection

Demo controls expose Third person beside Resume on desktop and in the headset menu header. The Controls page also has an inspection card. The existing inspection driver owns orbit, zoom and return: desktop uses a separate camera, while VR keeps its tracked first-person camera and shows a stationary mirror. Inspection suspends input and restores the previous state when closed.

Death snapshots keep the exact posed skin (CPU skinning for the readable procedural rig), mesh scale and material colors and source textures at birth. Jaguar rosettes survive the initial fall, then dissolve into the source coat pigment. NPC elimination also creates the detached corpse before the original enemy is removed. The floor hint from gameplay cannot clip or reposition the snapshot. The flat procedural bone layout is connected into 43 cosmetic joint links using its bone names; hierarchical rigs retain their existing links. A detached visual skeleton falls under gravity and bends at its actual joints for at least 1.4 seconds. Scenery sweeps prevent the cosmetic body from falling through solid surfaces. It creates no rigidbodies, colliders or gameplay forces.

Liquefaction waits for settling (with a five-second limit for bodies falling off the island). Then the fallen pose flows into a puddle over 4.5 seconds, stays wet for 1.25 seconds, and dries over 2.75 seconds. Its support point follows translating moving scenery. This is visual ragdoll simulation; it does not change respawn timing, death rules or networking. Several simultaneous deaths still need Quest performance testing.

Explicit verification: DevTools Editor MeltControlChecks.Play(session, check, optionalCapture) checks an actual avatar snapshot, preserved dimensions at birth even with an invalid floor hint, zero initial melt, collider-free ragdoll, delayed gradual liquefaction, wet hold, cleanup and inspection restoration. It is not run automatically. The combined UserFeatureChecks runner captures the supplied stages and exercises actual NPC elimination. Batch inspection uses the real VR mirror with a synthetic VR control mode because a hidden batch editor cannot capture the desktop cursor; it restores the original mode afterward. A physical headset test remains required.

Ragdoll constraints still simulate at 30 Hz. Geometry is presented once per rendered frame using cached joint and local matrices, with a fast path for vertices influenced by one joint. Rigid accessories follow joint transforms; translating support moves the settled snapshot without uploading its vertices again. The Breakwater Village eight-bot earthquake checks measured median Editor frames of 269 ms before these changes and 33 ms afterward; average deformation time fell from 342 ms to 5.7 ms. Collapse outcomes and corpse counts vary between runs. These diagnostics do not predict headset performance. The full-size fall, textures, gradual melt, wet hold and cleanup checks passed after the optimization. Reports are in Logs/Validation/BreakwaterProfileBefore.txt and BreakwaterProfileAfter.txt.

The combined Game Play Mode run verified full-height skinned geometry, retained vertex/index counts, more than 30 joint constraints, bounded corpse dimensions, gradual liquefaction, wet hold, cleanup and actual NPC elimination. Birth, fall, partial-melt and puddle captures were visually reviewed. Collision sweeps ignore initial-overlap contacts with undefined hit points so a corpse far from world zero cannot be pulled to the origin.

## Local death paint

Local elimination uses CandyDeathPaint instead of the liquid curtain. It takes the dying player's avatar color from the existing actor/frame, independently of underwater and round-end tints. A 0.16-second outward impact forms irregular wet lobes, scattered droplets and paint fingers; drips settle briefly, then a curved wipe clears them between 1.15 and 2.2 seconds. The shader generates its surface grain, glossy highlights and wet edges in one transparent pass, without particle objects or gameplay physics. Duplicate elimination/frame notifications are suppressed.

The desktop overlay follows the camera aspect ratio. VR uses the same eye-stable world-space panel with stereo shader macros at 1.1 metres. Underwater, sinking, heat and round-end curtains retain their original material. Actual headset comfort and visual coverage still require a headset test.

An explicit DeathPaintChecks request renders impact, settled, wiping and cleared GPU previews under Logs/Validation and checks shader support and complete clearing. These previews are shader checks, not actual gameplay captures.
