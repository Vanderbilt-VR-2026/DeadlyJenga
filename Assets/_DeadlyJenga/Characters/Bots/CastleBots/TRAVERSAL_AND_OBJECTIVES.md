# Bot traversal and mode objectives

The host supplies `BotObjectives.Current` through Core. Providers append world-space goals, urgency, target identity, radius, and expiry. They describe opportunities; they do not move bots or award outcomes. Characters chooses among them using personality, distance, and a short commitment bonus. Attack requests use the same mode-authorized punch path as a player. Evade goals become retreat destinations. Objective switches wait until a grounded decision point; they never clear an active jump, drop, climb or vault landing.

Navigation samples upward-facing world faces, including rotated and inverted blocks. Walk, ballistic jump, drop, vault intercept, and short climb links are checked against actual collision geometry. Climb routes require real reachable faces through a bounded seam search and use ordinary held-climb input. Stamina, moving-support release, carrier restrictions, and enemy ownership apply normally. Bots can recover and replan; unreachable destinations are not fulfilled through teleports.

Shared geometry snapshots are refreshed periodically. Expensive graph edges have a rotating global budget (20 per frame by default), local fallback sensing has a separate rotating budget (8 per frame), and crowd queries use spatial cells. These are CPU bounds, not a claim that 100 bots already meet a Quest frame budget. New movement abilities still need an `ICastleBotTraversalAbility` adapter to explain reachability and ordinary controls.

`BotNavigationValidation.RunBatch` tests real gap crossing and climbing a four-metre tower with 45-degree yaw and a course seam. `ModeBotAcceptance.RunBatch` uses Game's real mode runtime and eight normal bots on a small physical tile map; it checks flag captures, block ownership/points, and damaging infection conversions. The latter is objective acceptance, not a substitute for castle/cathedral or real-peer tests. Reports retain failed routes and objective counters. Tests place initial fixtures normally and never teleport bots mid-route or invent scores.

Hand opponents now approach horizontally with fingers toward the structure and vary between poking, slapping, punching, scraping, and friction-grip attempts. The shared physical hand remains responsible for all contact forces; grip attempts do not create target joints or rewrite block velocity. Attack phase flags are read-only diagnostics. The sustained hand regression still requires at least ten real successful contact attacks, forty displaced castle blocks, and six active attackers; it is not passed by attack animation alone.

The current horizontal-hand regression passed the sustained castle thresholds. Four ordinary hands also made ten successful physical attacks in seventeen attempts on the actual magnetic Evan Demo 2 map over sixty seconds. Dual sea-spirit hands each pushed a physical test block; replica previews did not simulate contact.

Desktop sampling on the actual 560-block territory map measured mean frame times of 2.78, 7.61 and 15.44 ms for 10, 50 and 100 bots; corresponding 95th percentiles were 4.05, 10.70 and 20.99 ms. This was a rendered editor run with sleeping blocks, no voices, no active disasters and no flying hands. It is not a chaotic-scene or Quest capacity measurement. Headset comfort, arbitrary navigation geometry, real multiplayer latency and device thermal performance still need acceptance testing.

Flag carriers retain the normal slow movement and vault rules and cannot climb. Low-ceiling launches use precise staging points. Bounded searches preserve real escape alternatives when a route must first move away from the goal. If a high destination needs another approach, carriers can select a public, standing-clear stair stage and travel there through the same controller. Walkable foundations use short raycast- and capsule-verified walking stages; sinking islands do not use this fallback. Fire, incoming meteors, tornado reach and waves contribute a shared half-second risk cache rather than per-bot state captures.

Actual-map acceptance includes a natural capture with twelve bots on the 788-block CTF map while six default-scheduled disasters occurred; the capture happened near the end of the bounded 600-second run. A separate exact ground-start carrier reproduction physically crossed the island and climbed the stairs to its 36-metre home in about 61 simulated seconds, with no climbing or later teleports. KOH bots reached and scored on the 64-metre hill after six supported bridges made its approach accessible. Actual 688-block Infection produced another infected participant under a default-scheduled tornado. These demonstrate completed objectives, not perfect tactics or guaranteed round duration. The later defender-only guard adjustment is covered by the mode-role regression; it does not turn the twelve-bot capture sample into a statistical win-rate guarantee.
