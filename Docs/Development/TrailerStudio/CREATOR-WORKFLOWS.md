# Trailer Studio creator workflow research

Reviewed November 22, 2026 against Studio source at `cb1c158145bf0a6fedcee2a844b51ad4efb8cc41`. This is a product and production review. Suggested workflows below are proposals. Cost ratings describe relative engineering effort. All proposals can use existing Unity editor code, local media and offline processing. Commercial products are references.

## The first three creator workflows

### 1. Mark a moment and cut between real angles

In Shoot, select a cue, an observed gameplay event or a manually marked frame. Choose **Compare angles**. Show the chosen player's POV, a witness and an establishing camera at the same source frame. Scrub all three together, keep one angle, or cut from one to another at that frame. Preserve the take, source range, camera and event identity when a cut is reopened.

Start with two angles and three stills per angle around the event. Existing cameras should be usable on day one. Add cheap moving previews and suggestions for clear cameras after the source-time contract works. If the authored cue precedes actual contact, label both and default to observed contact when available. A cue that never fired stays visibly unconfirmed. Offer a manual marker for a quiet glance or reveal.

**Proof:** On one unchanged tape, cut from a player's takeoff to the witness view of the same landing. Reopen the Set and identify the same source frames and people. Change the world take and verify the old selection remains identifiable. The image sequence should let an unfamiliar viewer say who acted and what changed. Resolve's Sync Bin, Epic's recording workflow and Rockstar's marker stills are direct precedents. [Resolve Cut](https://www.blackmagicdesign.com/products/davinciresolve/cut/) · [Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/recording-animation-using-take-recorder-in-unreal-engine) · [Rockstar](https://www.rockstargames.com/newswire/article/51974aa3a7o218/rockstar-editor-updates-coming-in-september-to-ps4-xbox)

### 2. Direct groups of people and preserve the hero

In Set, select a few actors or draw a starting area and make a named group. In Perform, isolate that group, leave prior groups playing, and choose one solo performer when needed. In Polish, drag a destination or reaction point, preview paths and stagger reactions by distance. Give the user **Keep this actor** and **Try another background variation**. Store stable member IDs and the seed. Removing, renaming or adding someone should preserve approved actors and their clothes.

Begin with groups, solo, lock, an outfit preview, and a dry-run placement count. Reuse crowd generation. Next add ground and path sampling at representative frames, reporting the first unsafe location. Offer fewer valid placements when a requested crowd cannot fit. A newly registered map can use a supported walkable surface and user-picked focus point; an empty or unsupported anchor must be visible before recording.

**Proof:** Stage 32 people across three groups. Re-record one group, swap two outfits, and verify the other groups' take IDs and the hero's appearance stay the same on disk and on screen. Repeat on a narrow elevated map and a broad map. Inspect feet at a later frame as well as frame zero. [Epic groups and tags](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/cinematic-tags-and-groups-in-unreal-engine) · [Layered recording](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/record-gameplay-in-unreal-engine?lang=en-US) · [Blender action timing and blending](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.5/editors/nla/sidebar.html)

### 3. Save a scene that can be cast and staged again

Start with reusable roles such as hero, witness and approaching hazard, plus one movable stage origin. Save relationships such as “witness faces hero” and “camera sits on this side of the action.” On another catalog map, choose the roles, click a stage point, and preview the marks, sightlines and camera reversal before creating a new Set. Preserve chosen cosmetics independently from camera roles. Some maps lack a supported surface, room, or the necessary hazard. Explain which requirement failed and let the creator reposition the scene. World physics must be captured afresh on the destination map.

**Proof:** Apply the same reveal on two geometrically different maps, swap the hero, and retain the camera's intended subject. An unregistered or incompatible map produces an actionable reason, and a tape captured on another map cannot become this map's physics history. [Epic transform origins](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/creating-level-sequences-with-dynamic-transforms-in-unreal-engine?lang=en-US) · [Epic role binding](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/dynamic-binding-in-sequencer)

## Fix or measure before expanding the editor

**Retime and game-sound sync.** The FCPXML writer in [StudioExport.cs](../../../Assets/_DeadlyJenga/DevTools/Editor/TrailerStudio/Engine/StudioExport.cs) computes the game-sound offset from shot start plus trim-in and explicitly assumes real-time playback. A speed ramp or freeze can invalidate that assumption. Export a known flash and click at normal speed, half speed, and on each side of a freeze; inspect the resulting timeline in the receiving editor. Specify separately what should stretch, continue or mute during a freeze. This is a source finding; the size of any audible error has not been measured.

**Measure the work saved.** Time a creator finding one impact, producing two angles, and replacing the second angle after feedback. Count repeated recordings and full renders. In a crowd scene, count how many actors need manual repair after changing the map. Compare these with the current workflow on the same prompt. Use normal-speed viewer review for action clarity and real rigs for costume continuity.

**Scope.** Keep the six tabs and put each action beside its existing owner. Start with synced angle review and take provenance, then group controls and reference frames. Add route rehearsal and portable scene templates after stable IDs and map checks are in place. Leave elaborate grading, spectral repair and full editorial finishing to the receiving editor. These recommendations do not depend on a cloud model or paid asset library.

## Two 10-second trials

- **Tsunami chase.** Establish the intended escape route in POV. Let surf arrive just before the first witness cut. Match the player's takeoff across the two angles, then hold the witness shot long enough to see both the player land and the crossing disappear. If the water hides the player in every view, change the witness camera and reuse the take.
- **Quiet reveal.** A player pulls a block aside and discovers another survivor wearing the same outfit. Show the pulling hand, carry the scrape across the cut, then hold both players in one frame. Use a pinned reference to match the costumes and the direction of the block's motion. This tests comic timing and continuity without relying on destruction or rapid cuts.
