# 360 camera walk (optional)

A 360 camera films in every direction at once. Walking a path with one
covers a lot of campus fast. **The plan works with only a phone.** Buy one
only if you want faster coverage.

## Settings

- **Mode:** 360 video. **Resolution: the highest (8K).** 30 fps is fine.
- **Exposure:** fixed for the walk if the camera allows (set ISO and
  shutter in Pro mode, then leave them). If you must use auto outdoors,
  pick an overcast day so it barely changes.
- **White balance:** fixed (for example Cloudy or 5500K), not Auto.
- **Color:** Standard. No filters, no HDR video, no "adaptive tone".
- **Keep the raw `.insv` files.** Never delete them after exporting.
- **Stabilization and "horizon lock":** these rotate the picture to keep
  it level and smooth. That is fine, but it means the picture no longer
  matches how the camera was actually held. The raw `.insv` keeps the
  original plus the motion data, so the computer can choose later. If you
  ever export an `.mp4`, export one with direction lock on and write down
  which settings you used.

## Gray card

At the start of each walk, hold the gray card about 1 m from the camera,
facing the lens, for 3 seconds.

## How to walk

- **Pole straight up and down.** The camera stays level and upright.
- **Two height passes** where practical:
  - **about 1.0 m**: stick held low, about hip height;
  - **about 1.7 m**: stick held up, above your head.
- **Speed: about 1 m per second.** A slow, steady walk. No jerks, no fast
  turns. Turn corners in a wide curve.
- **Distance:** about 1 m or more from walls; never closer than 30 cm.
- **Loops:** start with a loop around the area, then fill the middle with
  overlapping lines. **End where you started** (a "loop closure") so the
  computer can check its own drift.
- **Where you hide:** you are always in a 360 video. Hold the stick
  straight out, keep your body directly under or beside it, and keep your
  face turned down. The intake script blurs faces anyway.
- Keep walls and doorways out of the seam between the two lenses (the
  sides of the camera) when you can; point a lens at the important wall.

## Short clips

Record one clip per area (a few minutes each), not one hour-long file.
Say the area name out loud at the start of each clip.
